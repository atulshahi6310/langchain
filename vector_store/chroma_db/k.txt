# Krishna and Arjuna: The Wisdom of Duty

**Characters:**

* Narrator
* Lord Krishna
* Arjuna

---

**Narrator:**

The great battle of Kurukshetra was about to begin. Two mighty armies stood facing each other. On one side were the Pandavas, fighting for justice. On the other side were the Kauravas, fighting to keep their kingdom. Arjuna, one of the greatest warriors, asked his friend and guide, Lord Krishna, to drive his chariot to the center of the battlefield.

As Arjuna looked around, he saw his teachers, elders, cousins, and friends standing on both sides. His heart filled with sorrow.

---

**Arjuna:**

Krishna, my hands are trembling. My mouth is dry. I cannot hold my bow. These are not strangers before me. They are my family, my teachers, and my loved ones.

How can I fight against them? Even if we win the kingdom, what joy will remain if those we love are gone? I would rather give up everything than become the cause of their death.

---

**Krishna:**

Arjuna, your compassion is noble, but this sorrow has clouded your judgment. You are seeing only the body, not the eternal soul.

The soul is never born, and it never dies. It cannot be destroyed by weapons, burned by fire, drowned by water, or carried away by the wind. Bodies come and go, but the soul continues its journey.

Why grieve for what is eternal?

---

**Arjuna:**

Even if the soul is immortal, I still feel guilty. I do not wish to fight my own relatives. My mind is confused, and I do not know what is right.

Please guide me. I am your student.

---

**Krishna:**

Listen carefully, Arjuna.

Every person has a duty in life. As a warrior, your duty is to protect righteousness and stand against injustice. If good people abandon their responsibilities because of fear or attachment, then evil will triumph.

Perform your duty without hatred and without selfish desire.

Your responsibility is to act, not to control the outcome.

---

**Arjuna:**

But how can I work without worrying about success or failure?

---

**Krishna:**

That is the secret of true wisdom.

Focus on your actions, not on the rewards. Success and failure are temporary. When your mind remains calm in both victory and defeat, you become free from fear and disappointment.

A wise person performs every action with sincerity but leaves the results to the Divine.

---

**Arjuna:**

My mind is restless. It jumps from one thought to another like the wind. How can I ever control it?

---

**Krishna:**

The mind is indeed difficult to control, but it is not impossible.

With regular practice, self-discipline, and detachment, the mind becomes steady. Just as a lamp burns steadily in a place without wind, a disciplined mind remains peaceful even during difficult times.

Meditation, patience, and self-control are the paths to inner peace.

---

**Arjuna:**

What happens to those who dedicate themselves to goodness and righteousness?

---

**Krishna:**

No good action is ever wasted.

Even the smallest act of kindness, honesty, or devotion brings positive results. A person who sincerely follows the path of righteousness never truly fails.

Life offers many opportunities for growth, and every sincere effort moves the soul closer to wisdom.

---

**Arjuna:**

Who is the truly wise person?

---

**Krishna:**

A wise person remains calm in happiness and sorrow, gain and loss, praise and criticism.

Such a person treats everyone with kindness and respect. They do not let anger, greed, pride, or jealousy control their decisions.

Their happiness comes from within, not from wealth or power.

---

**Arjuna:**

People follow many different paths. Which one is the best?

---

**Krishna:**

There are many paths that lead toward truth.

Some seek wisdom through knowledge.

Some serve others through selfless action.

Some worship with devotion and love.

Some meditate to understand themselves.

When these paths are followed sincerely and without selfishness, they all lead toward the same ultimate reality.

---

**Arjuna:**

Krishna, you speak with such confidence. Who are you truly?

---

**Krishna:**

I am present in every living being.

Whenever righteousness declines and injustice becomes powerful, I manifest to protect the good, guide humanity, and restore balance.

Everything in this universe exists through Me, yet I remain beyond it.

---

**Narrator:**

To help Arjuna understand the greatness of the Divine, Krishna revealed His universal form.

Arjuna saw countless faces, countless stars, the sun, the moon, mountains, rivers, and the entire universe existing within Krishna. Time itself seemed to stand still.

Filled with awe and devotion, Arjuna folded his hands.

---

**Arjuna:**

O Lord, now I understand that You are beyond human understanding.

Forgive me for treating You only as my friend. I now see Your divine nature.

Please return to Your gentle form, for my heart cannot bear this magnificent vision.

---

**Krishna:**

Do not be afraid.

Remember that faith, humility, compassion, and selfless action are the foundations of a meaningful life.

Stand firm in your duty.

Act with courage.

Speak the truth.

Help others.

Keep your mind devoted to goodness.

That is the path to peace.

---

**Arjuna:**

My doubts have disappeared.

My fear is gone.

With your guidance, I now understand my purpose.

I will perform my duty with courage, without attachment to success or failure.

---

**Narrator:**

Strengthened by Krishna's wisdom, Arjuna picked up his bow once again. His heart was no longer filled with fear but with clarity and determination.

The battle of Kurukshetra began, but the teachings shared between Krishna and Arjuna became timeless. For thousands of years, they have inspired people to live with courage, wisdom, compassion, self-discipline, and faith.

The message remains the same even today: perform your duty with honesty, remain humble in success, stay strong in adversity, and always choose righteousness over injustice.

**End**
